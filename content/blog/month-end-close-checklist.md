Month-end lives in spreadsheets, bank feeds and a dozen "quick question" emails, and the close pack is still due Monday. A clear checklist won't make the work disappear, but it shows exactly which steps are judgment and which are just preparation.

## 1. Collect

- [ ] Bank and credit card statements for every account
- [ ] Payment processor payouts (Stripe, Square, PayPal)
- [ ] Payroll reports
- [ ] Bills and receipts received during the month
- [ ] Open questions from the client or business owner

**Hand off:** gathering statements and exports, and listing what's still missing with a drafted request to the client.

## 2. Reconcile

- [ ] Bank accounts to statements
- [ ] Payment processor payouts to bank deposits, net of fees and refunds
- [ ] Credit cards
- [ ] Accounts receivable and payable aging
- [ ] Payroll to the general ledger

**Hand off:** the matching itself. An agent can tie payouts to deposits, so that 214 transactions are matched and only the handful that don't tie out are shown.

## 3. Review exceptions

- [ ] Uncategorized or miscategorized transactions
- [ ] Unmatched deposits and payments
- [ ] Unusual amounts versus prior months
- [ ] Refunds and credits booked to the right period

**Keep with a person:** deciding what each exception means. Let the agent flag them; you decide.

## 4. Adjust

- [ ] Accruals and prepaid expenses
- [ ] Depreciation and amortization
- [ ] Deferred revenue
- [ ] Intercompany entries

**Keep with a person:** approving and posting entries. An agent can draft the support; you post.

## 5. Report

- [ ] P&L, balance sheet and cash flow
- [ ] Variance notes against last month and budget
- [ ] Overdue invoices and collections status
- [ ] Close pack sent to the client or owner

**Hand off:** drafting variance notes and the collections summary, with every number cited to the ledger. **Keep:** the sign-off.

## Where to start

Start with reconciliation. It's the most mechanical and the most time-consuming, and the results are easy to check.
