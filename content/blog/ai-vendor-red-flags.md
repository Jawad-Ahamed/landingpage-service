Every vendor now says its AI is "secure" and "enterprise-grade". Firms bound by confidentiality need more than adjectives. These are the ten warning signs we'd look for, whoever you buy from, including us.

## 1. "We don't train on your data"… but only verbally

If the no-training promise isn't in the contract or the published terms, and doesn't cover every model provider the tool uses, it isn't a promise. Ask which providers sit behind the product and get each one's terms in writing.

## 2. Compliance badges without paperwork

"HIPAA compliant" and "SOC 2" are claims you can check. Ask for the SOC 2 report under NDA, the date of the audit and its scope. Ask whether they'll sign a Business Associate Agreement. A vendor who can't answer is telling you something.

## 3. Answers with no sources

If you can't click from an answer to the document, page or ledger entry it came from, every answer has to be checked from scratch. That's slower than doing the work yourself, and riskier.

## 4. It acts on its own

Tools that email clients, post entries or submit forms without a human approving them move liability onto you. Look for drafts that wait for approval, and a clear "needs review" state.

## 5. One login for the whole firm

Shared accounts mean shared access: everyone sees every client. Per-person connections keep one partner's inbox from becoming the firm's inbox, and make audit trails meaningful.

## 6. "Just upload your files"

Bulk uploads copy client data into yet another place. Connections through each app's own sign-in (OAuth) read data where it lives, with access you can revoke.

## 7. Passwords requested

No modern tool should ask for your QuickBooks, Google or Microsoft password. If it does, stop there.

## 8. No clear exit

Ask what happens when you leave: are tokens revoked, is data deleted, and on what timeline? "We'll handle it" is not an answer.

## 9. Pricing you can't predict

Usage-based AI pricing is normal, but you should be able to see your usage and know what happens at the limit, especially before busy season.

## 10. It's built for everyone

General tools are great for general work. For tax prep, matter summaries or prior auths, look for workflows that understand your documents, your systems and your review process.
